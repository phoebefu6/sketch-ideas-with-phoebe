Your revenue pipeline has a departure time. It's whenever the one person who understands it goes home.

Software engineers have a blunt name for this: the bus factor - how many people can leave before a system stops. So I drew our industry's most common answer as the thing it's named after. A bus timetable.

Route 01, revenue pipeline: one qualified driver. Route 04, churn model: one. Route 09 says two, but the second driver left in March and nobody updated the sign - that's how bus factors decay, silently.

And Route 12, the pricing engine? Service withdrawn. Sole driver resigned. Nine years of discount logic left with them.

The line I want every data leader to sit with: a route only one person can drive is not a service. It is a favour.

Three things I now check on any team I lead:
- Count drivers per route, not headcount per team. Ten people and ten bus-factor-1 systems is not resilience, it's ten favours.
- Treat the "2 that's really 1" as the dangerous case - it looks covered on the org chart.
- Fund the second driver BEFORE the resignation. Onboarding takes quarters. Route 12 had nine years.
