# Structured content - bus factor comic

## Title
BUS FACTOR

## Subtitle
the number of people who can leave before a system stops

## Learning objective
Reader with zero engineering background understands what bus factor means, why a bus factor of 1 is dangerous, and the three ways to raise it - in under 10 seconds.

## Panels (4, read left-to-right, top-to-bottom, 2x2 grid)

### Panel 1 - "Meet Sam"
- Scene: cheerful office. One cartoon person (Sam, friendly, glasses) at a desk beside ONE humming machine labeled "PRICING SYSTEM". Coworkers in background chatting, not looking at the machine.
- Caption box: "Only Sam knows how the pricing system works."
- Mood: warm, everything fine.

### Panel 2 - "Sam gets on a bus"
- Scene: Sam waving goodbye, holding a cardboard box, stepping onto a cute hand-drawn bus. Speech bubble from Sam: "Bye!"
- Caption box: "One day, Sam leaves."
- Mood: still cheerful - the danger not yet visible.

### Panel 3 - "Nobody can drive it"
- Scene: same machine now with a red ERROR light and a sad face drawn on its screen. Three panicked coworkers around it, question marks over heads. One holds a sticky note that says "password??"
- Caption box: "The system is still there. Nobody can run it."
- Mood: comic panic.

### Panel 4 - "The lesson"
- Scene: calm flat card, minimal. Big text: "BUS FACTOR = how many people can leave before a system stops." Below, smaller: "If the answer is 1, you don't have a system - you have a Sam." Bottom line with three small icons: "Raise it: document - pair - rotate."
- Mood: deadpan takeaway.

## Data points
None - definitional content only, no statistics.

## Design instructions
- All text within centre safe area (survives 1.35:1 centre-crop from 4:3).
- Minimal text per panel - short captions reduce raster-text garble risk.
- One red accent only (panel 3 error light); everything else warm craft pastels.
