Worth a Thousand Words · Chart No. 01
Plates I–III · 1998–2024

The Price of a Word

What the market paid for a name it could not verify
Three measured plates on one question: is the market pricing the business, or the word?
The same word. Opposite actions. Both paid.
Adding “.com” was worth 74 per cent. Deleting it was worth 64 per cent. The word was the asset in both directions.

The same instrument, a new word

  • Walk — AI expertise measured from employee resumes — predicts later AI patent quantity and quality.
  • Talk — investment claims made on the earnings call — does not. Within a firm, past talk does not forecast future walk.
  • The market rewards talk in the short run and discounts it in the long run. Walk earns large, persistent gains only over longer horizons.
Plate III carries no madder. The 2016–2024 study reports direction, not a published abnormal return, so no return is stamped here. The receipts for this word are not in yet.

Madder. A published cumulative abnormal return. Stamped twice on this sheet and nowhere else.

Indigo. A count of the word being said. Not a payment.

Hatch leans with the change: right for a word added, left for a word removed.

The market has never required the thing. It prices the sentence.
