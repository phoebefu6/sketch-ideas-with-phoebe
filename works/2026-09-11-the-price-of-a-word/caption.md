In 1999, adding ".com" to your company name was worth about 74 per cent.

After the crash, removing it was worth about 64 per cent.

Same word. Opposite actions. Both paid. That is not a joke from a conference talk, it is two
peer-reviewed event studies, and I drew them as two plates on one sheet so you can see the heights
next to each other.

The detail that stopped me was in the first paper. The authors sorted the 95 firms by how much
business they actually did on the internet - pure internet companies, firms with some prior
involvement, firms that pivoted, and firms whose core business had nothing to do with it - and the
announcement-day effect was similar across all four groups. A mere association was enough. The
market was not pricing a business it could inspect. It was pricing a sentence.

So I added a third plate, because the instrument is running again. The share of US public firms
saying "AI" on the quarterly earnings call went from roughly zero in 2016 to about 20 per cent by
mid-2024.

And here is the part I want the data people to sit with. A 2025 working paper builds two measures
for the same firms: AI talk, taken from what managers claim on the call, and AI walk, measured from
AI expertise in employee resumes. Walk predicts later AI patents. Talk does not. Within a firm,
past talk does not forecast future walk. The market rewards talk in the short run and discounts it
in the long run.

Which means the useful question is not "are we in an AI bubble". It is: is my roadmap buying talk
or buying walk? Both get paid. Only one of them is still paid in five years, and only one of them
shows up in what my team can actually build on a Tuesday.

One thing I deliberately did not do: put a number on the AI plate. That study reports direction,
not a published abnormal return, and the colour on this sheet means "a figure someone published".
So the third plate carries no accent at all. The receipts for this word are not in yet.

Every figure is quoted as published, the sources are printed on the work, and no company is named.
Not investment advice - a note about language.
