# LinkedIn caption draft - data infrastructure town

The data world named its own architecture after geography, then forgot to draw the map.

Lake. Warehouse. Mart. Lakehouse. Mesh. Five terms that get thrown around interviews and vendor decks - and they make instant sense the moment you take the names literally:

- Data LAKE - the pond where everything lands raw. Files, images, logs, decide-later.
- Data WAREHOUSE - the big tidy building. Cleaned, structured, shelf-ready for reports.
- Data MART - the corner shop next door. One team's curated slice of the warehouse.
- Data LAKEHOUSE - the house on stilts at the water's edge. Warehouse comfort, built directly on raw lake storage.
- Data MESH - not a building at all. Four garden plots, each with its own well and its own owner, connected by contract.

The question that actually matters in an architecture review isn't "which one is best" - it's "who owns the water quality." In the first four, a central team does. In the mesh, every gardener does.

Drew the whole town so you never have to nod through a lakehouse conversation again.

Which one does your company actually run? (Most run three of these at once and call it a strategy.)
