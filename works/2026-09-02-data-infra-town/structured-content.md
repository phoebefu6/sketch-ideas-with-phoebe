# Structured content - data infrastructure town

## Title
WHERE DOES YOUR DATA LIVE?

## Subtitle
five buildings, one town - the names were the map all along

## Learning objective
Reader can tell the five architectures apart by their literal geography: what each stores, how structured it is, and who owns it - in one look.

## Scene (single isometric hand-drawn town)

### 1. DATA LAKE (large pond, left side)
- Visual: big friendly lake with raw, mismatched things floating in it - a file, a photo, a log scroll, a music note. Slightly murky but cheerful.
- Label: DATA LAKE
- One-liner: "store everything raw - decide later"

### 2. DATA WAREHOUSE (big building, centre)
- Visual: large tidy warehouse building, wide open door showing neat identical boxes on shelves in rows.
- Label: DATA WAREHOUSE
- One-liner: "cleaned and structured for reports"

### 3. DATA MART (small shop, next to warehouse)
- Visual: cute corner shop with striped awning, small shelf of a few curated boxes, one happy customer.
- Label: DATA MART
- One-liner: "one team's curated slice"

### 4. DATA LAKEHOUSE (house on the lake shore)
- Visual: cozy house built on a deck OVER the lake edge - stilts in the water, tidy shelves visible inside.
- Label: DATA LAKEHOUSE
- One-liner: "warehouse comfort on raw lake storage"

### 5. DATA MESH (four small wells in four garden plots, connected by pipes)
- Visual: four mini neighborhood plots, each with its own small well and owner figure tending it, joined by a network of pipes/paths.
- Label: DATA MESH
- One-liner: "each domain owns its data - shared by contract"

## Data points
None - definitional only.

## Design instructions
- Style traits from Phoebe's pasted reference: macaron pastels, wobble outlines, doodle stars, hand lettering, cream paper.
- All text inside centre safe area (1.35:1 crop contract).
- 12 short labels total - garble-safe load.
- Lakehouse must visibly touch BOTH the lake and dry land - that adjacency is its whole meaning.
- Mart smaller than warehouse and near it - a mart is a subset.
