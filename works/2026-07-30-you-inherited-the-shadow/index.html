The making of

Key Concept

A forecast is a distribution, but executive reporting often turns it into one crisp point estimate. The approved number looks clean. The omitted interval still exists, and it becomes the risk the business has to operate inside.

Explanation

The poster turns forecast uncertainty into a graphic physical system. The signed $4.23M card is the point estimate executives approve. The widening dark fan is the prediction interval expanding across the planning horizon. The ruled grid makes time visible. The acid-lime marker shows the actual commitment sitting deep inside the widest part of the range.

The lesson for data and AI people: uncertainty is not decoration. If a model output drives planning, resourcing, inventory, budget, or headcount, the range is part of the decision. Hiding it does not make the forecast clearer. It only transfers the uncertainty to the team that has to execute.

Design-Built Recipe

This final artifact was hand-authored as HTML/SVG and rendered with Chrome so the grid, labels, forecast value, interval fan, and caption stay precise.

Build a 4:5 portrait poster about forecast uncertainty as a graphic measured shadow. Use a warm planning-paper ground, near-black ink, graphite interval bands, and one acid-lime accent. Place a crisp signed "$4.23M" point-estimate card near the lower-left foreground, rotated along a diagonal planning axis. From that point, draw a large dark prediction-interval fan widening toward the upper-right across a ruled quarterly grid. Layer interval bands and dot/line texture so the fan reads as measured risk, not decorative shadow. Put one acid-lime commitment marker deep inside the widest part of the fan. Use compressed heavy sans typography for: "You approved the number. But the company lives in the range." Keep monospace labels and tabular numerals. Finish with the bottom caption: "Approve the range, not just the value."
