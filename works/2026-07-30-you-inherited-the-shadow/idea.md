# You approved the number. You inherited the shadow.

## Key Concept

A forecast is a distribution, but executive reporting often delivers it as one point. The point is the crisp object. The prediction interval is the widening shadow the business actually has to operate inside.

## Explanation

The poster turns a forecast into a physical system. The freestanding `$4.23M` object is the board-ready point estimate. The widening dark fan is the prediction interval expanding across the planning horizon. The ruled surface makes time visible. The violet flag shows the actual commitment sitting far inside the uncertain zone.

The lesson for data and AI people: uncertainty is not decoration. If a model output drives planning, resourcing, inventory, budget, or headcount, the range is part of the decision. Hiding it does not make the forecast clearer. It only transfers the uncertainty to the team that has to execute.

## Design-Built Recipe

This final artifact was hand-authored as HTML/SVG and rendered with Chrome so the numeral, grid, horizon, interval fan, and caption stay precise.

```text
Build a 4:5 portrait poster about forecast uncertainty as a physical measured shadow. Use a dark ruled quarterly planning surface under one hard directional light. Place a small freestanding "$4.23M" point-estimate object near the lower foreground. Let it cast a dark fan-shaped prediction interval that widens toward the upper-right. Place one violet commitment flag at the far, soft edge of the interval. Keep the point estimate crisp and neutral. Reserve the only chromatic accent for the business commitment inside the uncertain zone. Bottom-anchor one restrained sans caption: "You approved the number. You inherited the shadow." Make the visual feel calm, forensic, expensive, and boardroom-credible.
```
