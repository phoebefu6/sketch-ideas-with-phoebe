The model can't spell strawberry

Data portrait · 2026-07-18 · two-color acid

LLMs read tokens, not letters - which is why they confidently miscount the r's in strawberry. The unit of thought isn't the character you see.

The recipe

The full prompt, published as promised - copy it and make something.

Flat vector editorial data-portrait poster, crop 4:5. STRICT two-color system: acid lime #D2FF00 and near-black #111112 ONLY - no white, no gray, no third color, no gradients. Near-black background. Concept: an LLM confidently miscounts letters because it reads tokens, not characters. HERO: a giant acid-lime '2' (the model's confident answer) with a bold strike-through slash, and a smaller correct '3' beside it. Top-left, tiny mono-caps question: 'HOW MANY R'S IN STRAWBERRY?'. Below the hero, the word 'strawberry' broken into token tiles [str][aw][berry], with the three r's circled inside the tiles so the eye hunts and finds them. Bottom: one thin acid-lime hairline rule with caption 'It reads tokens, not letters. That's why it can't spell.' Massive negative space, Swiss grid discipline, the joke lands in two seconds.
