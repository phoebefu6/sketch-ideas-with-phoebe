The making of

Key concept

"What share of the economy has adopted AI?" has at least three honest answers, and in late 2025 they were 18, 41 and 78 per cent - in the same country, in the same few weeks. A Federal Reserve note from April 2026 lays them side by side:

ruler what is counted US figure source
firms share of firms that used AI 18% Census BTOS, Dec 2025
workers, own use share of workers using genAI for work 41% Real-Time Population Survey, Nov 2025
workers, by roof share of the workforce at a firm that has adopted AI 78% Atlanta Fed Survey of Business Uncertainty, Nov 2025

Nobody is wrong. They counted different things on the same street.

Explanation

The picture paints that street. Two office towers fly the flag - the firms that use AI - and their windows are full of people at screens. Around them, small shops: a baker, a barber, a bookseller, a florist, a bike mechanic, a tailor, a toymaker, all working by hand. One cafe owner sits at a laptop, and her shop flies no flag.

Three surveyors measure the same street:

  • Count the firms - a clicker on each door. Most doors are small shops, so the rate is small: 18 per cent of US firms.
  • Count everyone under a flag - a tape round the two towers, taking in every desk inside whether that person uses AI or not: 78 per cent of the US workforce works at an adopting firm.
  • Count who uses it - a ladder and a lantern, window by window, including the cafe: 41 per cent of US workers use genAI for work.

The order is not an accident. Big firms adopt first - 37 per cent of US firms with 250+ employees used AI in May 2026, against under 20 per cent of the smallest (Census) - and big firms hold most of the workers. So a firm-weighted rate is small, an employment-weighted rate is large, and individual use sits in between, pulled up by people using their own tools where the company says it does not.

The practical rule for any dashboard: an adoption rate without its unit is not a number yet. Say per firm, per worker, or per worker-at-a-firm - then the date.

Recipe

  • Maker: raster, GPT Image through codex-cli, 2:3. Four variants. The first two put a laptop in nearly every shop, which blurred the argument; the third fixed that but carried almost no information; the fourth builds the facts into the street - a shop sign and a tower plaque for WHY the rates split, and a source line on each surveyor's signpost.
  • Style: storybook high street - watercolour and gouache, ink outlines, autumn blue hour, lettering on the banner, a hanging shop sign, a brass plaque, a cafe chalkboard, the three signposts and the scroll - every fact in the scene, no paragraph block.
  • Why it works as a picture: the weighting argument is abstract in words and obvious in a place. Two towers are two firms and most of the people at once, and the surveyor with the tape is visibly measuring something different from the one with the clicker.

Sources: J. S. Allen, "Monitoring AI Adoption in the U.S. Economy", FEDS Notes, Federal Reserve Board, 3 April 2026. US Census Bureau, "Large Firms With at Least 20 Employees Biggest AI Users", May 2026.

SCENE: a cobbled autumn high street at soft blue-hour twilight, curving gently uphill, lamps glowing. In the middle distance stand two tall glass office towers, each flying a small orange flag; their many windows glow with people working at screens. A brass plaque on the base of the towers reads exactly: "BIG FIRMS, 250+ STAFF: 37% USE AI"
Along both sides are cosy small shops where people work by hand: a baker kneading bread, a barber cutting hair, a bookseller, a florist, a bike mechanic, a tailor, a toymaker - NO laptops or screens in these shops. A hanging wooden shop sign over the street reads exactly: "SMALL SHOPS, 1-4 STAFF: UNDER 20% USE AI"
Only ONE small cafe has a glowing window where the owner works on a laptop; a chalkboard outside the cafe reads exactly: "OWNER SAYS: NO AI HERE"

FOREGROUND: three friendly surveyors in waistcoats, each beside a large painted wooden signpost. Each signpost has four lines exactly as written:
1. Left - a surveyor with a clipboard and a hand tally clicker counts shop doors. Signpost:
   "COUNT THE FIRMS" / "18%" / "every door counts once" / "Census, Dec 2025"
2. Centre - a surveyor pulls an enormous yellow measuring tape wrapped around both towers. Signpost:
   "COUNT EVERYONE UNDER A FLAG" / "78%" / "users or not" / "Atlanta Fed, Nov 2025"
3. Right - a surveyor on a wooden ladder holds a lantern up to the cafe window. Signpost:
   "COUNT WHO USES IT" / "41%" / "cafe included" / "Worker survey, Nov 2025"
The percentages are the largest text on each signpost.

TOP: a painted ribbon banner across the sky reading exactly "18% OR 78%?" and beneath it in smaller hand lettering exactly "Same street. Same autumn. Three honest rulers."
BOTTOM: a curling paper scroll across the cobbles reading exactly "No unit, no number." and beneath it smaller: "Say per firm, per worker, or per worker-at-a-firm."

All lettering is clean, correctly spelled English, hand-painted and legible. No other text anywhere. No robots, no glowing brains, no circuit boards, no logos, no watermark, no dark background, no photorealism.
