The making of

Key concept

"What share of the economy has adopted AI?" has at least three honest answers, and in late 2025 they were 18, 41 and 78 per cent - in the same country, in the same few weeks. A Federal Reserve note from April 2026 lays them side by side:

ruler what is counted US figure source
firms share of firms that used AI 18% Census BTOS, Dec 2025
workers, own use share of workers using genAI for work 41% Real-Time Population Survey, Nov 2025
workers, by roof share of the workforce at a firm that has adopted AI 78% Atlanta Fed Survey of Business Uncertainty, Nov 2025

Nobody is wrong. They counted different things on the same street.

Explanation

The picture paints that street. Two office towers fly the flag - the firms that use AI - and their windows are full of people at screens. Around them, small shops: a baker, a barber, a bookseller, a florist, a bike mechanic, a tailor, a toymaker, all working by hand. One cafe owner sits at a laptop, and her shop flies no flag.

Three surveyors measure the same street:

  • Count the firms - a clicker on each door. Most doors are small shops, so the rate is small: 18 per cent of US firms.
  • Count everyone under a flag - a tape round the two towers, taking in every desk inside whether that person uses AI or not: 78 per cent of the US workforce works at an adopting firm.
  • Count who uses it - a ladder and a lantern, window by window, including the cafe: 41 per cent of US workers use genAI for work.

The order is not an accident. Big firms adopt first - 37 per cent of US firms with 250+ employees used AI in May 2026, against under 20 per cent of the smallest (Census) - and big firms hold most of the workers. So a firm-weighted rate is small, an employment-weighted rate is large, and individual use sits in between, pulled up by people using their own tools where the company says it does not.

The practical rule for any dashboard: an adoption rate without its unit is not a number yet. Say per firm, per worker, or per worker-at-a-firm - then the date.

Recipe

  • Maker: raster, GPT Image through codex-cli, 2:3. Three variants; the first two put a laptop in nearly every shop, which blurred the argument, so the third prompt says six of the eight shops work by hand.
  • Style: storybook high street - watercolour and gouache, ink outlines, autumn blue hour, lettering only on the banner, the three signposts and the scroll.
  • Why it works as a picture: the weighting argument is abstract in words and obvious in a place. Two towers are two firms and most of the people at once, and the surveyor with the tape is visibly measuring something different from the one with the clicker.

A tall portrait storybook editorial illustration, hand-painted watercolour and gouache on warm airy paper, delicate ink outlines, rich warm saturated colours, full-bleed painted scene to every edge, premium children's-book craft for grown-ups, optimistic and intellectually serious.

SCENE: a cobbled high street at soft blue-hour twilight seen straight on, like a stage set, the street curving gently uphill. Along it stand eight small cosy shops (a bakery, a barber, a bookshop, a florist, a bike repair shop, a tailor, a cafe, a toy shop) and two tall office towers. ONLY the two towers fly a small orange flag on their roofs. The towers are full of windows, and many of their windows glow warm amber. The small shops are cosy but ordinary: shopkeepers bake bread, cut hair, arrange flowers, sew, fix a bicycle - by hand, with NO laptops, NO screens, NO computers in six of the eight shops. Only TWO small shops have a single glowing window where one shopkeeper sits at a laptop - and these two shops fly no flag. The contrast must be obvious: towers full of lit screens, the small shops almost all hands-on work.

In the foreground, three friendly surveyors in waistcoats measure the same street in three different ways, each beside a painted wooden signpost:
1. Left: a surveyor with a clipboard and a hand tally counter counts shop doors one by one. Her signpost reads exactly: "COUNT THE FIRMS 18%"
2. Centre: a surveyor pulls an enormous yellow measuring tape wrapped around both tall towers, sweeping in every window lit or dark. His signpost reads exactly: "COUNT EVERYONE UNDER A FLAG 78%"
3. Right: a surveyor on a tall wooden ladder holds up a lantern and peers into the glowing windows one at a time, including the lit window of a small shop. Her signpost reads exactly: "COUNT WHO USES IT 41%"

TOP: a painted ribbon banner across the sky reading exactly: "18% OR 78%?" and beneath it in smaller hand lettering exactly: "Same street. Same autumn. Three honest rulers."
BOTTOM: a curling paper scroll across the cobbles reading exactly: "No unit, no number."

All lettering is clean, correctly spelled English, hand-painted, large and legible. No other text anywhere. No robots, no glowing brains, no circuit boards, no logos, no watermark, no dark background, no photorealism.
