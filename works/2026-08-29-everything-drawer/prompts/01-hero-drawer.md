Overhead flat-lay photograph, portrait, deep focus, everything sharp at once. The entire
contents of one immense old wooden specimen drawer laid out corner to corner and filling the
frame: thousands of small dull brass weights like watch parts and counterweights, coils of fine
copper wire, stacks of punch cards gone soft at the edges, tangled ribbons of paper tape, rows
of tiny plain brass dials and toothed gears, half knolled into careful rows and half collapsed
into loose piles. A dusty warm cast unifies brittle yellowed plastic, tarnished brass and
softened paper. One soft overhead light, short honest shadows, no drama.

AMONG the objects, exactly THREE clean rectangular empty outlines marked out with aged masking
tape, nothing inside them, only the bare green drawer felt showing through - the only empty
spaces in the whole frame, each large enough to hold something that is not there.

A worn wooden drawer-front rail strip runs across the very top edge of the frame, plain,
unlabeled, with two tarnished brass handles.

Museum reverence toward junk - a collection plate for things nobody would keep. No empty
background anywhere else, filled to the corners. Absolutely no lettering, no numerals, no
engraved words, no printed labels, no tags with writing, no logos, no stamps anywhere - every
word is composited afterwards in HTML.
